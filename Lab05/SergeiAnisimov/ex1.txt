Exercise 1 — Publisher and subscribers

An unnamed pipe passes bytes from the publisher to its child subscriber.
With multiple subscribers, the publisher sends a copy to each child.
Each child forwards its copy to a separate FIFO.

One shared FIFO would act as a shared byte stream. A read consumes bytes
from it, so readers compete for the available bytes. A separate FIFO for
each subscriber gives each reader its own stream.

On Ubuntu 22.04 under WSL, tests with one and three subscribers delivered
the same three messages to every subscriber, including a 1024-byte
message. The publisher removed its FIFOs after EOF and after SIGINT.
