Exercise 2 - Translation lookaside buffer

I kept Aging as the replacement policy and used the same 15 requests in
every configuration. I varied the number of virtual pages and RAM frames
to see how changing the TLB capacity and the available memory affected
the results. Run bash ex2.sh to reproduce the table on Linux or WSL.

The TLB keeps recent page-to-frame mappings. Its capacity is one fifth
of the number of virtual pages, rounded down, with one entry minimum
when the TLB is enabled. On a TLB miss, the MMU checks the page table.
When a page is evicted, I drop its cached mapping so that the TLB cannot
return an old frame number. The TLB miss ratio is misses divided by the
total number of requests.

Pages   Frames   TLB entries   TLB misses   Page hits   Disk accesses
5       2        1             13/15        4/15        13
10      2        2             11/15        4/15        13
10      3        2             11/15        7/15        10

With two frames, increasing the table from five to ten pages gave the
TLB two entries instead of one. The TLB miss ratio fell from 86.67% to
73.33%, but the page hit ratio stayed at 26.67%. With ten pages, adding
a third frame raised the page hit ratio to 46.67% and saved three disk
accesses. The TLB miss ratio stayed at 73.33%. This is possible because
a page can be absent from the TLB and still be present in RAM.
